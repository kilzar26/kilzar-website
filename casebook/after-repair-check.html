How do you know whether a car repair worked?

Keep a record of what changed before you call the problem solved. A code disappearing is one observation. The original symptom, test conditions, and follow-up matter too.

Keep four pieces of evidence together

  1. Before: Write the complaint in plain language. Record when it happened, any scan results, and the source of each observation.
  2. Action: Record the test or repair actually performed and the date or odometer reading.
  3. After: Repeat the relevant check when practical. Record whether the symptom returned, what the scan showed, and the conditions of that check.
  4. Status: Say “improved,” “unchanged,” or “not checked yet” if that is all the evidence supports. Leave a case open until a meaningful follow-up.

Example: a lean-condition code

Suppose a driver records P0174 and a rough idle. After work, a new scan has no recorded code. The useful follow-up is whether the rough idle changed and under what driving conditions the vehicle was checked. A blank scan field without a scan is unknown; a missing code on one scan is not proof that the underlying problem is gone.

Record only readings you actually have. An owner’s observation, a technician’s observation, and a scan tool reading should remain distinguishable.
