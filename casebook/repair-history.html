KRAI CASEBOOK / FIELD GUIDE

A vehicle repair history that answers “what changed?”

A list of replaced parts rarely tells the whole story. A useful record connects the original problem, the evidence, the work, and what happened afterward.

What to record for each visit

  • Vehicle and date: year, make, model, mileage, and when the concern appeared.
  • Problem: the driver’s words, the conditions that triggered it, and what was observed.
  • Evidence: code or measurement only when actually captured, with its source. Keep receipts or scan files separately if your tool cannot attach them.
  • Action: tests, parts, and labor that actually happened; avoid rewriting a guess as a confirmed cause.
  • Follow-up: the same symptom or test after the work, including how long or under what conditions the vehicle was checked.

Why the follow-up is worth keeping

When a concern returns months later, a chronological case helps you see which symptoms repeated, what was previously tried, and whether the earlier repair was ever verified. It also makes a handoff to a repair professional clearer. A record does not establish a diagnosis by itself; it makes the underlying observations easier to review.

Keep a backup. A record saved only in a browser can disappear when browser data is cleared or you change phones.
